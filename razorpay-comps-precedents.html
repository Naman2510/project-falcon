01 — Methodology

Set construction & calendarization

No public company matches Razorpay's exact mix (online payment gateway + banking-as-a-service + payroll + BNPL-adjacent rewards). The set below is built in three tiers by business-model proximity, and every figure is normalized to a trailing-twelve-month (TTM) window ending at each company's most recently reported quarter — not stale fiscal-year-end figures. This matters here specifically because one comp, Paytm (One97 Communications), runs a March fiscal year-end while every global peer runs December — a naive FY-to-FY comparison would line up numbers nine months apart. Rolling TTM windows re-base all six companies onto the same trailing calendar period regardless of fiscal-year convention.

Banker's note — Razorpay reports on an Indian FY (April–March) and discloses annual, not quarterly, financials. Its FY25 (ended Mar-2025) figures are therefore compared against peers' TTM windows ending mid-to-late 2026 — an unavoidable ~12–15 month lag for the one private company in the set. This is flagged, not hidden, in every calculation that uses Razorpay's financials below.

Why the un-weighted average is the wrong number — the plain six-name average (4.9x) is dragged down by Fiserv and Global Payments — mature, single-digit-growth acquirers with a fundamentally different risk/growth profile than a 65%-growth Indian platform business. Excluding those two as poor growth comps, the "narrow" peer average (Adyen + PayPal + Paytm) is 7.0x; excluding PayPal too (scaled, low-growth, U.S.-centric) leaves Adyen + Paytm at 9.75x — the more defensible read for a business growing revenue at 65% with a still-nascent India/EM international footprint. This is the multiple selection judgment call that matters more than the arithmetic.

03 — Precedent Transactions

Six transactions selected for relevance to a Razorpay scenario: scale consolidation (GPN/Worldpay), a best-in-class embedded-fintech-infra margin comp (Nasdaq/Adenza), two BNPL/product-expansion deals (Block/Afterpay, PayPal/Paidy), the single most relevant India payments precedent (PayU/BillDesk — including its collapse), and a recent strategic-optionality deal for next-gen rails (Stripe/Bridge).

04 — For Context

Razorpay's own M&A track record

Worth reading against §03: every large market precedent above is a scale or optionality play measured in billions. Razorpay's own eight acquisitions since 2019 are the opposite — small, capability-driven tuck-ins that bought product surface area (offline POS, payroll, core-banking tech, loyalty) rather than market share. That contrast is itself informative for the pitch's process recommendation: Razorpay has never run — and has no internal muscle memory for — a scale transaction of the kind seen in §03.

Read — trading comps and the Pine Labs precedent both sit meaningfully below the reported $5.0–6.0B DRHP price talk, while precedent-transaction control premiums sit above it. That's the normal, healthy pattern for an IPO of a scarce, high-growth platform asset: public trading comps and a recent direct-peer IPO set a conservative floor; a hypothetical strategic sale sets a much richer ceiling; management's actual price talk sits sensibly in between, closer to the floor than the ceiling — consistent with a company choosing the IPO path specifically because it believes public markets, not a single strategic buyer, will pay the fuller price. Recommended range for board discussion: $4.5B–$6.0B, i.e., the DRHP talk anchored down slightly toward the comps/Pine-Labs floor to build in IPO-market conservatism.
