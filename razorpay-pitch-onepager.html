1Situation Overview

  • Razorpay completed its India re-domiciliation in 2025 and confidentially filed IPO papers with SEBI in 2026, targeting a ₹5,000–6,000 Cr primary-plus-OFS raise.
  • FY25 revenue grew 65% YoY to ₹3,783 Cr ($427M) on $180B of processed volume; the company posted a one-time ₹1,209 Cr loss driven almost entirely by a ~₹1,275 Cr re-domiciliation tax charge — the core payments business was EBITDA-positive.
  • Reported price talk of $5.0–6.0B is a 20–33% cut to the 2022 peak private mark of $7.5B — in line with, not worse than, the broader India-fintech re-rating (Pine Labs listed Nov-2025 at ~35–45% below its own 2022 peak).
  • New tailwind: Parliament's Aug-2026 amendment reopening merchant-fee (MDR) charging on large-merchant UPI volumes — zero-MDR since 2020 — is a structural, not yet fully priced, revenue-pool unlock for every UPI-heavy PSP, Razorpay included.
  • Crowded window: PayU and PhonePe are both pursuing India listings on a similar timeline, competing for the same pool of IPO investor demand.

2Strategic Rationale for a Dual Track

Running a quiet parallel track alongside — not instead of — the IPO preserves optionality without disrupting either path:

  • Pricing tension: a credible strategic alternative is the single best lever against IPO investors anchoring to the depressed end of the comps range (see companion Comps & Precedents memo — trading comps alone imply a floor near $4.2B).
  • Window risk hedge: three India fintechs racing to list in the same 12-month window raises real execution risk; a strategic option is the fallback if the IPO market shuts or reprices adversely mid-process.
  • Precedent-transaction evidence: control buyers have paid 17–18x revenue for best-in-class fintech infrastructure (Nasdaq/Adenza) and even richer multiples for dominant India payment rails specifically (PayU/BillDesk, pre-collapse) — a strategic bid could plausibly clear the IPO range.
  • Discretion is the constraint, not the opportunity: BillDesk's own deal collapsed on regulatory timeline risk after 14 months — an India payments sale process is slow and leak-prone. That argues for 2–3 targeted conversations, never a broad auction.

Recommended board range: $4.5–6.0B — anchored to observable DRHP price talk, with the trading-comps/Pine Labs floor as downside discipline. A strategic bid inside 2–3 confidential conversations would only be actionable above the top of this range on all-cash, high-certainty terms (see §4).

4Process Recommendation

  1. Continue primary-track IPO prepPrimary — advance the confidential DRHP to public filing; target a listing window in H2 2026–2027 subject to market conditions.
  2. Run a narrow, confidential market checkParallel — 2–3 targeted, NDA-bound conversations with strategic acquirers for whom Indian UPI/BaaS rails close a real strategic gap; no broad auction, no data room, no leak surface.
  3. Single mandate structurePrimary — engage one bank as IPO lead-left and strategic-alternatives advisor so all sensitive information sits with one team.
  4. Pre-agreed off-rampParallel — the board pauses IPO filing only for an all-cash strategic offer clearing >1.3x the top of the DRHP range with a credible regulatory path; otherwise proceed to public filing on the current timeline.
